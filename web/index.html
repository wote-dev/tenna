Your phone's notifications,
answered from your Mac.

Tennanova is two small apps — one in the macOS menu bar, one on your Android phone — that talk straight to each other over a single encrypted connection on your own network. Notifications arrive on the Mac, replies go back to the app that sent them, and both machines share one clipboard.

What it does

Six things, done properly, instead of thirty half-finished ones.

  1. 01

    Notifications land where you already look

    Alerts appear in macOS Notification Center with the app's name and its icon. Group summaries, ongoing foreground-service clutter and anything you have muted never leave the phone, so the Mac stays quiet.

  2. 02

    Reply with a real keyboard

    Type into the notification on macOS and the text goes back through the notification's own reply field. It lands in the actual conversation, in the actual app — not a workaround, and not an SMS gateway.

  3. 03

    Press the buttons the app shipped with

    Mark as read, archive, snooze, mute — whichever action buttons a notification carries are all invokable from the Mac.

  4. 04

    Dismiss it once

    Clear it on the Mac and it clears on the phone. Clear it on the phone and it leaves Notification Center. You never triage the same alert twice.

  5. 05

    One clipboard, both directions

    Copy a link, a code or a paragraph on either machine and paste it on the other. One real image travels too, including image files copied in Finder; every image is checked against its advertised size and hash before it reaches your clipboard.

  6. 06

    Stays between your two machines

    A single TLS connection runs directly between the apps, pinned to your Mac's own key. If your router blocks device-to-device traffic, Tennanova keeps a USB tunnel alive on its own. There is no server in the middle and nothing to upload.

Set up once, in about a minute

Three steps, then it is quiet forever.

  1. 1

    Open the Mac app

    It appears in the menu bar and shows a pairing code the moment it launches.

  2. 2

    Scan it on the phone

    Tap Scan pairing code. The scanner is the system one, so Tennanova never asks for your camera.

  3. 3

    Confirm two Android grants

    Notification access, then Accessibility for clipboard capture. Coming back from each Settings screen moves you along by itself.

And what you never have to do

  • Install Shizuku, a daemon or a privileged bridge
  • Go through wireless-debugging pairing
  • Redo anything after a reboot
  • Create an account or hand over an email address

What it deliberately isn't

Left out on purpose, not waiting on a roadmap.

  • Screen mirroring
  • Webcam
  • Calls
  • SMS threads
  • File transfer
  • A cloud account

Tennanova does notifications and the clipboard, and it does them without a service in the middle. It is coming soon — private testing first.
